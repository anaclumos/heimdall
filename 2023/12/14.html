Google Fails to Provide Data Recovery: User Outraged by Impending Data Destruction​

  • Google did not provide a data recovery service before informing users about the deletion of their data, which has left an anonymous user disappointed.

Real-Time Exploration of NeRF Models Made Possible with SMERF​

  • SMERF is a new technique that enables real-time exploration of NeRF models directly in web browsers.
  • The method delivers exceptional accuracy and produces scenes that are nearly identical to offline models.
  • Users can experience the project on the official website and share their feedback through email or Twitter.

Reactions​

  • Google has created SMERF, a real-time rendering technology for exploring 3D models on different devices.
  • Feedback on the demos has been positive, but improvements are needed in movement and VR mode.
  • There are concerns about long loading times and large payload sizes, with suggestions for improvement.

Polish White-Hat Hackers Threatened by Train Manufacturer After Restoring DRM-Impacted Trains​

  • White-hat hackers in Poland successfully assisted an independent maintenance company in restoring functionality to a train that was rendered inoperable by its manufacturer.
  • The manufacturer, unhappy with the bypassing of their anti-repair mechanism, is threatening legal action against the hackers.
  • This case has sparked a debate about the rights of independent repair professionals and the ongoing struggle between manufacturers' control over their products and the ability of consumers to repair them. The legality of the hackers' actions is currently uncertain under European copyright laws.

Reactions​

  • The article explores different scenarios involving hackers, DRM, train manufacturers, and GPS spoofing.
  • It delves into ethical debates surrounding hacking, the right-to-repair movement, and the legal implications of reverse engineering in the EU.
  • Safety concerns and potential criminal charges related to train systems are also discussed, along with the exploration of GPS spoofing and its potential applications.

Nvidia RTX 4090 Outperforms Apple M1 Pro with MLX in Machine Learning Performance​

  • The author conducted performance tests comparing Apple's MLX framework for machine learning on Apple Silicon with Nvidia's RTX 4090 using the Whisper benchmarking tool.
  • The benchmark results showed that the Nvidia 4090 was 16% faster than the MLX framework.
  • However, when using an Nvidia optimized model, the transcript was generated in just 8 seconds, demonstrating the advantage of optimization.
  • The author also mentioned tests conducted on M2 Ultra and M3 Max, which showed similar speeds to the M1 Pro.
  • The author acknowledged the potential inaccuracy of the measurements but emphasized that the MLX framework performed well for a laptop.
  • The purpose of the tests was for the author's podcast search engine project.

Reactions​

  • The article compares the performance of Nvidia RTX 4090 and Apple M1 Pro with MLX using the OpenAI Whisper repo for machine learning tasks.
  • The discussion explores the optimization efforts of Nvidia, AMD, Intel, and Apple in machine learning hardware.
  • The consensus is that Nvidia GPUs tend to provide better performance for machine learning compared to Apple's M1 chips, but other factors and optimizations should be taken into account.

  • The blog post provides instructions on how to use the llamafile program for image summarization.

Dropbox Users Warned: Disable Third-Party AI Access​

  • He advised users to disable this setting in their account settings and tagged the European Data Protection Supervisor in the tweet.

Reactions​

  • Dropbox users are raising concern and frustration over the company's new AI features that scan and access user files without explicit consent.
  • Speculations arise regarding regional restrictions and the availability of privacy settings.
  • Users are exploring alternative options with stronger data security and encryption due to concerns over lack of transparency and control over their data.

Australia Bans Engineered Stone Due to Health Risks​

  • Australia has become the first country to ban engineered stone due to the increase of silicosis cases, a lung disease associated with working with the material.
  • The ban will come into effect from July 2024 in most states and territories.
  • The decision has received support from trade unions, health organizations, and personal injury law firms, while engineered stone manufacturer Caesarstone has expressed disappointment and criticized the reasoning behind the ban.

Reactions​

  • The discussion revolves around worker safety and health risks associated with engineered stone and silica exposure.
  • The potential ban on engineered stone in Australia is considered, as well as the need for improvements in worker safety and compliance.
  • Studies have shown that low-silica engineered stone products can cause inflammation, challenging the notion that reducing silica alone will eliminate disease risk.

  • The library currently works with Ptreads and is being developed for Win32 threads. Users can include it in their project or build a shared library. An example is provided to demonstrate its usage, along with information on related work and where to seek support.

Cortex A57: Performance and Power Efficiency for Nintendo Switch​

  • The Cortex A57 is Arm's first 64-bit capable core, used in the Nintendo Switch's Nvidia Tegra X1 for high GPU performance in a portable gaming console.
  • It has a modest branch predictor and caching system, a 48-entry instruction TLB, a unified register file, distributed scheduling, and two integer pipelines.
  • The A57 processor can handle four integer operations per cycle, but floating-point operations have high latency. It has limited address space, and optimization efforts can improve game performance on the Nintendo Switch and older platforms with integrated graphics.

Reactions​

  • The conversation delves into multiple aspects of the Nintendo Switch console, such as hardware bugs, design decisions, and processor choices.
  • Discussions about cost-saving measures, game rankings, and comparisons with other gaming devices are also featured.
  • The conversation touches on homebrew capabilities, PS Vita, graphics capabilities, and future console improvements, presenting a variety of perspectives and opinions.

Ubuntu 24.04 LTS Boosts Performance with Default Frame Pointers​

  • Canonical is implementing frame pointers by default in Ubuntu 24.04 LTS to enhance performance engineering.
  • This change will simplify profiling and debugging processes, providing more precise performance analysis data.
  • Although there may be a minor performance impact, the increased accessibility to performance insights outweighs this drawback.

Reactions​

  • Ubuntu 24.04 LTS will enable frame pointers by default for better debugging and performance on specific platforms.
  • There is a debate about the trade-off between performance improvements and issues with core dumps and debugging.
  • The use of higher registers on x86_64 CPUs and its impact on performance is discussed.
